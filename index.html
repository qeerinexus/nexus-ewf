        About this model

        Source logic

        The relative effect of each technology — solar PV displacing CCGT, BIGCC treated as carbon-neutral, the ~12% parasitic energy load of carbon capture, the negative-emission credit from BIGCC+CC (BECCS), and the water/fertilizer offset from biochar — follows the seven-scenario life-cycle comparison in Al-Ansari, T. (2016), "Development of a Nexus Framework for the Assessment of Water, Energy and Food Systems," PhD thesis, Imperial College London, which modelled Qatar's energy–water–food nexus under a domestic food-production target. The uploaded document was used directly to derive these technology-effectiveness ratios (Chapters 6–10).

        Current data anchors

        Baseline magnitudes are recalibrated to public 2024–2026 figures rather than the thesis's 2016 estimates: solar capacity and Al Kharsaah / Ras Laffan / Mesaieed LCOE bids (QatarEnergy, TotalEnergies, pv magazine), Kahramaa peak water production and RO/thermal desalination mix, the National Food Security Strategy 2030 self-sufficiency targets (Ministry of Municipality), and the 2030 Nationally Determined Contribution of a 25% GHG reduction versus business-as-usual (Ministry of Environment & Climate Change / UNFCCC).

        What this is — and isn't

        This is an illustrative planning-support model: it reproduces the direction, relative magnitude and interaction effects the thesis identifies (e.g. PV cutting nexus GWP by roughly a quarter, full BECCS integration cutting it by >95%, carbon capture adding a natural-gas penalty even as it cuts GWP) scaled onto current national baselines. It is not a certified engineering cost model or a substitute for a full mixed-integer optimization of the kind the thesis's superstructure was built to support — treat absolute dollar and tonnage figures as indicative, and revisit with QatarEnergy / Kahramaa / MME engineering data before any investment decision.
